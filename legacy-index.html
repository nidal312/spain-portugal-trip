The route

Barcelona, then Valencia, then Lisbon

This is the spine to keep. Barcelona is the priority. Valencia is the “rest of Spain” stop that is actually close — about three hours by direct train, walkable, markets and the City of Arts, not a museum marathon. Lisbon plus a Sintra day is the right first look at Portugal.

Why not Madrid?

The AVE is slightly faster and Madrid–Lisbon has more flights, but Madrid is too big for one night and leans toward palaces and museums. Use it only if the Valencia–Lisbon flight had failed you. It didn’t: a cheap evening Ryanair leaves a full Thursday in Valencia.

Why not Porto?

Lisbon–Porto is about three hours by train. Adding it would hollow out Lisbon and Sintra. Porto’s signature visits also include port-wine cellars, which this trip is skipping. Save it for another time.

The transfer trick

There is no useful midday Valencia–Lisbon flight on Thursday 26 November. The 20:05 Ryanair (€23 pp) is the one to take: a full day in Valencia, land in Lisbon at 20:45 local, go straight to the hotel. The 05:55 TAP wastes the night; the 18:15 TAP is €194.

The week

Day by day

Evenings start early: sunset is around 17:15–17:40. Grouped by neighbourhood. Monday is built around the only Sagrada slots left — Park Güell first, basilica after lunch.

Sunday 22 November · Barcelona

Arrive, then Ciutat Vella

Morning Vueling lands 09:20. Metro or Aerobus to the Eixample or Born, drop bags. Sunset 17:26.

Afternoon Gothic Quarter and El Born on foot — cathedral and Santa Maria del Mar from the outside, tight streets, then Port Vell or Barceloneta if energy lasts. Skip the Picasso Museum.

Evening Halal dinner in the Gothic Quarter or El Raval. Early night. Sagrada is not available today.

Monday 23 November · Barcelona, north-east

Park Güell, lunch, then Sagrada

Sagrada has no morning tickets left on this date, so the day runs south from the hill rather than the other way around.

Morning Park Güell at 10:00 (or 10:30). Those slots were already down to last tickets. The park is yours once you are in — no need to rush out.

Lunch Walk down through Gràcia. Rasa Malaysia on Passeig de Sant Joan sits on this exact axis, between the park and the basilica, and states that its meat comes from a halal supplier.

Afternoon Sagrada Família at 13:15, 13:30 or 13:45 (€26). Then walk Avinguda Gaudí to Sant Pau Recinte Modernista next door (self-guided €17 after 14:00, €18 before; Nov–Mar 9:30–17:00). santpaubarcelona.org

Evening Optional sunset at the Bunkers del Carmel (free; be there before 17:26). Or stay in Gràcia for a quieter walk.

Tuesday 24 November · Montserrat, then one Gaudí house

Mountain morning, Passeig de Gràcia later

Morning Early FGC R5 from Plaça Espanya to Monistrol, then the rack railway (about 1h30 door to sanctuary). Official FGC + rack return from Barcelona, 4 zones, is €28.80 adult through 31 Dec 2026. vol.cremallerademontserrat.cat · turistren.cat. If the mountain is fogged in, switch to Girona (Avant about 40–50 min; fares not pulled).

Afternoon Sanctuary, viewpoints, Sant Joan funicular if it is clear (adult return €17.50). Head back mid-afternoon.

Evening One Gaudí interior, not both: Casa Batlló around 16:30–17:00, or La Pedrera Essential at 16:00–16:30 (rooftop, last slot 17:00). Passeig de Gràcia façades are free either way.

Wednesday 25 November · Barcelona to Valencia

A last morning, then the train

Morning Boqueria or a last neighbourhood walk, check out, Barcelona Sants. Aim for the 10:15 high-speed train (a nearby midweek service arrived Valencia Joaquín Sorolla at 13:13). Reconfirm on Renfe — that timetable was verified for Tuesday 10 November, not locked for the 25th. The 08:15 is the backup if you want a longer afternoon.

Train times and fares for Wednesday 25 November were not pulled from a live Renfe checkout. Aggregators showed a midweek pattern of direct trains in about 2h47–3h27, with “from” fares in the mid-€20s to mid-€30s. Book soon; prices are dynamic.

Afternoon Check in, Ciutat Vella. Mercado Central, La Lonja from the outside (interior ticket price not pulled), Torres de Serranos, old-town streets.

Evening Walk the Turia gardens to the City of Arts and Sciences for blue hour (sunset 17:40). Halal dinner in Ruzafa or the centre. Leave Oceanogràfic for Thursday, if at all.

Thursday 26 November · Full Valencia day, evening flight

The city in daylight, Lisbon after dinner time

Morning City of Arts grounds, or start at opening if you want the Oceanogràfic (price not pulled). Or more of the old town and La Lonja.

Afternoon Malvarrosa seafront or a slower café stretch. Leave for the airport mid-to-late afternoon for the 20:05 Ryanair.

Evening Land Lisbon 20:45 local. Metro or taxi to the hotel, nearby dinner only. No sightseeing push. Sunset already gone (17:16 local).

Friday 27 November · Lisbon

Viewpoints, the river, jumu’ah if you want it

Morning Optional jumu’ah at the Lisbon Central Mosque, Rua da Mesquita 2 (Praça de Espanha, Blue Line), then downhill through Parque Eduardo VII and Avenida da Liberdade. Or start in Alfama if you will pray closer to the hotel.

Afternoon Alfama lanes, Santa Luzia / Portas do Sol / Graça, Sé from the outside, down to Baixa and Praça do Comércio. Tram 28 is usually too packed to be worth the wait.

Evening Halal dinner in Mouraria or Martim Moniz, a short walk from Baixa. Time Out Market is a poor “night out” here — it is heavy on alcohol.

Saturday 28 November · Sintra, Cascais only if there is time

An early palace, a short day

Daylight is only about 9h45 (sunset 17:16). Start early. Pack lunch from Lisbon — Sintra has almost no reliable halal sit-down.

Morning CP train from Rossio to Sintra (~40 min). Pena Palace at 10:00, Essential Visit €20. Book here.

Afternoon Sintra town, then either the Moorish Castle or Quinta da Regaleira if you pre-book (prices not pulled).

Late afternoon Cascais only if you are ahead of the clock. Otherwise go back to Lisbon. If it rains, swap this day with Belém and keep Pena for a clearer window if tickets allow — or stay in Lisbon (Chiado, a Tagus sightseeing cruise, not a drinks cruise).

Sunday 29 November · Belém, then home

The river in the morning, the airport after lunch

Morning Belém: Jerónimos from the outside (interior only if pre-booked; price not pulled), Torre de Belém exterior, the riverfront. Pastéis de nata are usually egg, butter and sugar — generally treated as permissible — but some Portuguese pastries use lard, so ask at smaller bakeries. Expect a queue at Pastéis de Belém.

Afternoon Bags, metro to LIS. Do not add a new neighbourhood. For the 19:00 Transavia to Eindhoven, leave the centre mid-afternoon; for the 19:30 to Schiphol, the same shape of day.

Halal-friendly

Where to eat

Almost all of these are owner-declared, not certified by Instituto Halal. Ask about the meat source and whether alcohol is served. Jacqueline in the Eixample names a certifier but is a jazz club and cocktail bar — skip it.

Barcelona

  • Café Arabia — Gothic Quarter, near La Rambla. Middle Eastern; site states halal. cafearabiabarcelona.com
  • Kapadokya — Gothic Quarter, and a Barceloneta branch. Turkish; site states halal meat.
  • Rasa Malaysia — Gràcia, Passeig de Sant Joan, between Park Güell and Sagrada. Site names supplier Alimentbarna S.L.
  • Restaurante Árabe Halal Marrakech — Sants, near Plaça Espanya (useful before or after Montserrat).
  • El Raval (Himalaya, Adil Tandoori, Bismillah) is the densest kebab and Pakistani cluster, near the Tariq mosque — ask on the spot.

Source: Halal Rihla, Nov 2026.

Valencia

  • Beirut King — Ruzafa, Mossèn Femenia 12. Listing
  • Kunafa by Afandi — Ciutat Vella, C/ de Sant Martí 3.
  • French Corner (shawarma) — Ciutat Vella, C/ del Hospital 18.

These Valencia names come from secondary listings. Confirm halal status in person. Traditional paella often includes pork or unsourced meat — do not assume a “seafood paella” is fine without asking. Horchata is fine.

Lisbon

  • Taste of Pakistan — Mouraria, Rua São Pedro Mártir 37B.
  • Ghoroa — Mouraria, Travessa Benformoso 2.
  • Zaafran — listed as halal-certified Indian, Rua de São José 35.
  • Cantinho do Aziz — Mouraria, Rua de São Lourenço 5 (Mozambican, claimed).
  • Mezze — Arroios, Mercado de Arroios.
  • Zubir Churrasqueira — Roma-Areeiro, Rua do Zaire 40B (also on the Instituto Halal de Portugal hospitality page).
  • Ali Baba Kebab Haus — Rua da Palma 25.

Cluster around Martim Moniz / Mouraria, a short walk from Baixa. Grilled fish with olive oil and lemon is usually fine; ask sem vinho / sem porco. Avoid francesinha, chouriço, presunto, cozido. Source: HalalSpy, Oct 2026.

Hotels

Where to stay

Barcelona, 3 nights

Dreta de l’Eixample, around Passeig de Gràcia or toward Sagrada / Verdaguer — central, metro-rich, quieter than the Ramblas. El Born if you want atmosphere (it can be noisy); Gràcia for a local feel near Park Güell. Avoid basing on La Rambla itself.

Valencia, 1 night

Ciutat Vella / El Carmen so Wednesday afternoon and Thursday are on foot. Ruzafa if you prefer the food streets and will walk or metro 15–20 minutes.

Lisbon, 3 nights

Baixa, Chiado, or Avenida da Liberdade / Marquês de Pombal — flatter, metro to the airport and to the Central Mosque, easy to Mouraria. Alfama is beautiful but steep and awkward with Sunday luggage. Príncipe Real is a calmer uphill option.

Late November

Weather and daylight

Climate normals, not a forecast. Spain is on CET (UTC+1); Portugal is on WET (UTC+0), one hour behind — that is why a 20:05 Valencia departure lands at 20:45 in Lisbon. Maghrib is early; plan viewpoints for mid-afternoon. A full moon falls on 24 November 2026 (Weather Spark moon table).

Barcelona

Highs about 15–17°C, lows 7–9°C. A wet day about 14–16% of the time. Jacket weather; the sea is too cool for a swim.
